
- **Although** it was raining, we went for a walk.

- I went to school **even though** I had a headache.

- **Despite (-ing)** being nervous, she gave a great presentation.

- We enjoyed the picnic **in spite of** the bad weather.
 
- He passed the exam **in spite of (-ing)** having little time to study.

- I wanted to go out. **However**, I decided to stay home.

- The task was difficult. **Nevertheless**, she completed it on time.

## These **contrast expressions** show that two ideas are unexpected or contradictory, but they differ in **grammar and position**.

 | Expression | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **Although + subject + verb** | **Although** it was raining, we went for a walk. |
| **... even though + subject + verb** | We went for a walk **even though** it was raining. |
| **Despite + noun** | **Despite** the rain, we went for a walk. |
| **Despite + -ing** | **Despite raining**, we went for a walk. |
| **... in spite of + noun** | We went for a walk **in spite of the rain**. |
| **... in spite of + -ing** | We went for a walk **in spite of it raining**. |
| **... . However, ...** | It was raining. **However,** we went for a walk. |
| **... . Nevertheless, ...** | It was raining. **Nevertheless,** we went for a walk. |

--- 
### Key differences

 - **Although** and **even though** are followed by a **full clause**:

  > Although **she was tired**, she continued working.\
  > She continued working even though **she was tired**.

 - **Even though** is generally **stronger/more emphatic** than **although**:

  > Although he was ill, he went to work.\
  > Even though he was very ill, he went to work.

 - **Despite** and **in spite of** are followed by a **noun, pronoun, or -ing form**, not normally a complete clause:
  > Despite **the rain**, we went out.\
  > Despite **being tired**, she continued working.\
  > In spite of **the rain**, we went out.

 - **Despite** and **in spite of** have essentially the **same meaning**. **In spite of** is a little longer but otherwise very similar.

  > Despite the problem, we continued.\
  > In spite of the problem, we continued.

 - **However** and **nevertheless** connect **two sentences/ideas** rather than introducing a subordinate clause:

  > The task was difficult. **However,** we completed it.\
  >  The task was difficult. **Nevertheless,** we completed it.

 - **Nevertheless** is more formal and means roughly **“despite this” / “even so.”**

  > The evidence was limited. **Nevertheless,** the researchers continued their investigation.

 ### Important grammar point

 ❌ **Despite it was raining, we went out.**\
 ✅ **Despite the rain, we went out.**\
 ✅ **Despite the fact that it was raining, we went out.**\
 ✅ **Although it was raining, we went out.**

 So, a useful rule is:

 **Although / even though \+ subject + verb**

 **Despite / in spite of + noun / -ing**

 **However / nevertheless + new sentence**
