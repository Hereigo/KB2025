### Idioms

- The thief was **caught red-handed** while trying to steal a wallet.

- **She’s still green at** this job, but she’s learning quickly.

- He **was green with envy** when his friend bought a new car.

- I drank too much last night and **completely blacked out**.

- He’s **the black sheep** of the family because he never follows the rules.

- My grandmother **has green fingers**  ; her garden is always beautiful.

- After the breakup, she **had the blues** for weeks.

- The company **is in the red** after losing several big clients.

- We go out for a fancy dinner only **once in a blue moon**.

- **Out of the blue**, she called me after ten years.

- He **saw red** when he realized someone had scratched his car.

- I **told a white lie** about liking the food so I wouldn’t hurt her feelings.

---
| Idiom | Meaning (Simple Explanation) |
| --- | --- |
| **be caught red-handed** | Be caught while doing something wrong. |
| **be green** | Be inexperienced or new at something. |
| **be green with envy** | Be very jealous of someone. |
| **black out** | Lose consciousness or memory for a short time. |
| **black sheep** | A person who is considered different or a disgrace in a family or group. |
| **green fingers** | Be good at gardening. |
| **have the blues** | Feel sad or depressed. |
| **in the red** | Owe money; be in debt. |
| **once in a blue moon** | Something that happens very rarely. |
| **out of the blue** | Something that happens suddenly and unexpectedly. |
| **see red** | Become very angry. |
| **white lie** | A small or harmless lie, often told to avoid hurting someone’s feelings. |
